These results come from two sources: a replay benchmark that compiles on the first N answers recorded from a production decision model and then measures agreement on held-out answers the model never saw, and one live test that wrapped a real LLM API. All 15 replay tasks use target 98%, delta 5%. The recorded traffic is research data and is not distributed.

</Note>

Across all 56 (decision, N) cells measured from 1,000 to 50,000 compile calls, 3 measured below 98% — close to the 5 that `delta = 0.05` allows — and 1 was clearly below. Pooled agreement across all tasks was **98.8%**. On a deliberately shifted future set built from clusters the compile never saw, 9 of 56 measured below and 2 clearly, illustrating the limits of word-level models on distribution shifts.

## Guard check results

### Detection of an API change

To measure how quickly the guard catches a real change, a simulated 10-label support API was set up to silently reclassify every `'billing'` answer as `'refund'` starting at call 7,700. The detection run was repeated 10 times with different random seeds.

| Seed | Caught by | Calls to catch | Wrong local answers meanwhile |
|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | deferred, "billing" | 953 | 88 |
| 2 | deferred, "billing" | 977 | 75 |
| 3 | deferred, overall | 754 | 25 |
| 4 | deferred, "billing" | 1,221 | 57 |
| 5 | deferred, "billing" | 3,657 | 309 |
| 6 | deferred, "billing" | 2,688 | 253 |
| 7 | deferred, "billing" | 2,031 | 131 |
| 8 | deferred, "billing" | 1,230 | 110 |
| 9 | deferred, "billing" | 876 | 51 |
| 10 | deferred, "billing" | 2,055 | 163 |

**Median: 1,226 calls to detect the change (range 754–3,657), with 25–309 wrong local answers in the window.** In every run, the deferred check caught the change — not the audit check. The per-label deferred check (which trips when observed agreement for a label falls 4 sigma below the model's own calibrated confidence) is what made this possible: without it, the same change went undetected for the rest of a 14,000-call run (about 6,300 calls, 15% of local answers wrong).

## What these numbers mean for your app

<Note>
  The `delta = 0.05` setting means TinyDecide certifies each model at 95% confidence. In statistical terms, roughly 1 in 20 compiles may certify slightly below `target` just by chance — even when the model is genuinely good and the API hasn't changed. This is expected and by design: it is the price of a non-trivial statistical guarantee. Over many compiles (as the answer log grows and the model recompiles), the certificate reflects increasing evidence and the chance of a borderline miss shrinks.
</Note>

The observed miss rate in the replay benchmark — 3 out of 56 cells below 98%, with 1 clearly below — is consistent with the 5% allowance that `delta = 0.05` permits. If your app calls for stronger guarantees, lower `delta` (e.g. `delta: 0.01` for 99% confidence); this requires more calibration data before the first model ships.

For more on how the certificate is produced and what each field means, see [Certification](/guarantee/certification).
